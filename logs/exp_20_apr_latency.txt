--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. Uncontended lockdep cost (1 thread): lockdep adds +6 ns to lock and
   +9 ns to unlock. Total pair overhead: 15 ns (1.4×). This is a 3×
   reduction from the Mar 24 result (+23/+22 ns, 2.2×), confirming that
   the per-thread RB implementation has a much lighter uncontended path.

2. Lock added latency no longer grows super-linearly. At 64 threads, the
   added pair cost is 2,725 ns (1.3×), compared to 46,202 ns (5.8×) in
   the Mar 24 experiment — a 17× reduction in added delay. The g_meta_lock
   spinlock contention that caused the previous super-linear scaling has
   been eliminated by the per-thread RB approach.

3. Overhead is nearly constant across all thread counts at ~1.3×. This is
   the defining improvement: the previous implementation degraded from
   2.2× at 1 thread to 5.8× at 64 threads. The new per-thread RB backend
   scales proportionally with the baseline contention.

4. Lock vs unlock asymmetry is reduced. At 64 threads: lock adds +2,333 ns,
   unlock adds +392 ns (6:1 ratio). In the previous implementation the
   ratio was similar but absolute values were 16× higher (lock: +38,604 ns,
   unlock: +7,598 ns).

5. At 2 threads, lockdep lock latency (143 ns) equals the baseline (143 ns),
   meaning the instrumentation overhead is entirely hidden by the natural
   mutex contention. This confirms the per-thread design avoids adding
   serialization to the lock path.
