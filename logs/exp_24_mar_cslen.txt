--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. The overhead curve has three distinct regions:

   Region A — lock-thrashing (cs_ns 0–30): overhead is ~3–4×. The
   critical section is shorter than lockdep's own metadata cost
   (~250 ns), so lockdep dominates the per-acquisition time.

   Region B — crossover (cs_ns 50–200): overhead drops steeply from
   2.8× to 1.0×. This is where the application hold time begins to
   match and then exceed lockdep's metadata cost. The crossover to
   ≤1.5× happens at ~75 ns; near-unity overhead is reached at ~200 ns.

   Region C — work-dominated (cs_ns ≥300): overhead fluctuates
   around 0.9–1.3×, within measurement noise. The application's own
   serialization completely dominates; lockdep is invisible.

2. The critical crossover point is ~75–100 ns. Any application whose
   average lock hold time exceeds ~100 ns will see less than 1.5×
   overhead from lockdep. This is a much more optimistic threshold
   than the earlier coarse sweep suggested (which showed 1.5× at
   100 ns but had no data between 0 and 100 ns).

3. Below ~30 ns, the baseline itself is barely affected by the
   busy-spin (12.4M → 12.0M ops/s for 0 → 10 ns), confirming that
   very short critical sections are dominated by the lock/unlock
   cost itself, not the in-section work.

4. Lockdep throughput is relatively stable in 0–100 ns range (3.2M →
   2.1M ops/s), declining only 35%. In contrast, baseline throughput
   drops 75% (12.4M → 3.1M) over the same range. This confirms that
   lockdep's fixed per-acquisition cost acts as a "floor" that the
   baseline converges toward as hold time grows.
