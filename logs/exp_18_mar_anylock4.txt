--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. More locks does not uniformly reduce overhead. The 4-lock case is worse
   than 2-lock at ≥4 threads (8-11× vs 4-5×), despite offering more
   application-level parallelism. The reason: each iteration now issues up to
   4 trylock calls before giving up. Each successful trylock still acquires
   g_meta_lock internally, but the failed trylocks also touch lockdep state
   (slot lookup). With 4 locks the average number of lockdep operations per
   iteration is higher than with 2 locks.

2. Baseline throughput at 4 threads is higher for 4 locks (20.6M) than 2
   locks (10.9M) — 4 concurrent slots vs 2 reduces application blocking.
   But lockdep throughput at 4 threads is lower (2.3M vs 3.2M), confirming
   the overhead cost of additional trylock probes under g_meta_lock.

3. Diminishing returns from more locks for lockdep throughput: lockdep
   throughput across all thread counts converges toward ~1-2M ops/sec
   regardless of lock pool size, since g_meta_lock is the hard ceiling.

4. The crossover point matters: below ~2 threads, more locks are strictly
   better (less contention, same lockdep cost). Above ~4 threads, the extra
   trylock probes make 4 locks worse than 2 locks under lockdep.
