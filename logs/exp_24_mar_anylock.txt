Thread counts    : 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64
Iterations       : 100,000 per thread
Metric           : ops_per_sec  (total acquisitions / wall time)

Scenario
  2 shared locks. Each iteration a thread acquires whichever lock is free:
    1. pthread_mutex_trylock(lock[tid % 2])   — preferred lock (non-blocking)
    2. pthread_mutex_trylock(lock[1-tid%2])   — alternate lock (non-blocking)
    3. pthread_mutex_lock(lock[tid % 2])      — blocking fallback
  Allows up to 2 threads to proceed concurrently (one per lock).
  Differs from 1-lock high-contention: reduces serialization at the
  application level, so lockdep overhead is more visible in isolation.

Conditions
  baseline : no LD_PRELOAD
  lockdep  : LD_PRELOAD=./lockdep/liblockdep.so

lockdep limits (lockdep.h)
  LOCKDEP_MAX_LOCK_SLOTS      = 256
  LOCKDEP_MAX_HELD_LOCK_SLOTS = 64
  LOCKDEP_MAX_THREAD_SLOTS    = 128

--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. Overhead is stable with 2 locks (3-5×) vs degrading with 1 lock (4-12×).
   At 64 threads, 2-lock overhead is 4.8× vs 12.2× for 1 lock — a 2.5×
   improvement. The extra lock halves the application-level serialization,
   giving threads more opportunity to proceed without all queuing behind
   g_meta_lock.

2. Baseline throughput is lower for 2 locks than 1 lock (~10M vs ~15M
   ops/sec at ≥2 threads). The two trylock attempts before the blocking
   fallback add syscall overhead even when locks are uncontended; the 1-lock
   baseline avoids this by going straight to pthread_mutex_lock.

3. Lockdep throughput at ≥2 threads converges around 2-4M ops/sec
   regardless of thread count — the g_meta_lock spinlock caps throughput
   at roughly one lockdep metadata update per ~250-500ns. The 2-lock case
   does not eliminate this ceiling; it merely reaches it more gracefully
   because the application-level blocking is less severe.

4. The flat lockdep curve (no collapse at 32/64 threads unlike the
   low-contention case) confirms: the catastrophic overhead in low-contention
   multi-lock experiments is driven by both g_meta_lock AND the absence of
   any application-level throttling. Here, the 2 shared locks act as a
   natural rate limiter that prevents all threads from hammering g_meta_lock
   simultaneously.
