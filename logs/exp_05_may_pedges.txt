--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. Marginal lockdep cost scales with depth, not threads. Walking from
   (1, 64) to (64, 4), per-pair lockdep cost falls 47→3 ns under global
   and 26→4 ns under rb. The depth ratio is 16×; the global cost ratio
   is 15×. Confirms that per-acquire work is dominated by the O(depth)
   held-stack scan that checks each held lock against the predecessor
   summary for the lock being acquired.

2. Backend crossover is depth-dependent and lands between depth 8 and 16.
   - depth 64: rb wins by 21.5 ns/pair (global 47 vs rb 26)
   - depth 32: rb wins by 2.9 ns/pair
   - depth 16: rb wins by 1.5 ns/pair
   - depth  8: tied (~equal, both ~4 ns)
   - depth  4: global wins by 0.4 ns/pair
   The rb backend's gain from offloading the depth-N scan to a worker
   only outweighs its enqueue overhead at depth ≥ ~16.

3. The (1, 64) cell is the cleanest depth measurement: single-threaded,
   no scheduler/false-sharing noise, dependency edges populated once and
   skipped thereafter. Per-pair global cost of 58 ns at depth 64
   decomposes roughly as: ~10 ns base interception (matches the (64, 4)
   cell) plus ~0.75 ns per held-stack level (which extrapolates the
   linear fit across rows).

4. The (4, 64) cell is the worst case for global mode (498 ms, 6.3×
   baseline). This is the only cell that simultaneously saturates both
   lock-slot count (256) and held-stack depth (64). Adding threads at
   max depth multiplies total per-acquire scan work without adding
   parallelism on the lockdep side beyond the per-thread held-stack TLS
   cache. The rb backend handles this same cell in 336 ms (4.3×) — a
   33% wall-time reduction, the largest crossover gap in the table.

5. Predecessor summaries are doing the heavy lifting. Without them, the
   first iteration's O(depth²) edge installation would dominate total
   wall time at depth 64; we'd see 100k × O(depth²) graph work per
   thread, which at depth 64 would be ~410M DFS-step equivalents per
   thread. The fact that lockdep total wall is 374 ms for the (1, 64)
   single-threaded case means per-iter steady-state cost is 3.7 µs ≈
   58 ns × 64 acquires — matching the per-pair number, which means
   first-iter cost is fully amortized away.

6. Treat overhead *ratios* with care. The 6.3× ratio at (4, 64) looks
   worse than the 5.1× ratio at (1, 64), but the marginal cost in
   ns/pair is actually *lower* at (4, 64) (16 vs 47 ns). The ratio is
   inflated because adding 4 threads scales the baseline 3.7× while
   lockdep scales only 1.3× — lockdep is the more parallel-scalable
   side here, just not parallel enough to keep up with the baseline.

7. The (64, 4) cell oversubscribes 16 vCPUs by 4×. Baseline at this
   cell isn't measuring pthread fast-path cost; it's measuring
   scheduler thrash. The fact that per-pair lockdep cost still lands
   on the depth-extrapolated line suggests rb's worker thread is not
   meaningfully starved by oversubscription — the SPSC ring buffers
   absorb bursts well.
