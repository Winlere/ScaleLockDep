--- Key Findings ---

1. Marginal global cost scales near-linearly with held-stack depth:
     depth=4   -> 3.33 ns/pair
     depth=8   -> 4.46
     depth=16  -> 5.19
     depth=32  -> 8.31
     depth=64  -> 16-47 (single-thread vs contended)
   The slope (~0.2 ns per held-lock per acquire) is the cost of one
   held-stack scan + one predecessor-bit check per held lock.

2. rb wins at depth >= 32 and loses at depth <= 4-8:
     depth=64 (1T):   global 58.76 vs rb 37.83 ns/pair  (rb 36% faster)
     depth=64 (4T):   global 19.37 vs rb 13.05          (rb 33% faster)
     depth=32:        global 10.17 vs rb  8.05          (rb 21% faster)
     depth=16:        global  6.95 vs rb  6.20          (rb 11% faster)
     depth=8:         global  6.54 vs rb  6.04          (rb  8% faster)
     depth=4:         global  5.48 vs rb  6.02          (rb  10% slower)
   The crossover is near depth 4-8: below that, rb's per-acquire
   enqueue cost outweighs the saved O(depth) work; above, the worker
   thread's offload pays off.

3. The (1, 64) cell is the cleanest measurement of pure depth cost:
   one application thread, no contention, depth-64 stack. It shows
   global at 47 ns/pair marginal, rb at 26 ns/pair marginal — rb
   absorbs roughly half of the hot-path graph work into its worker.

The May 5 run on commit 3f06985 reported essentially identical

--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. Fixed per-call interception cost (TLS lookup, recursive guard,
   slot resolution, held-stack push/pop) is best estimated from the
   shallowest cell (64, 4): marginal global = 3.33 ns/pair, marginal
   rb = 3.87 ns/pair. That ~3 ns is the irreducible per-pair cost of
   intercepting pthread_mutex_{lock,unlock}.

2. Per-acquire scan cost in global mode grows linearly in depth, as
   expected: every nested acquire walks the held-stack to add edges
   (or skip via the predecessor-bit summary). Predecessor caching
   keeps this fast — without it the cost would be quadratic in depth.

3. rb's value proposition is depth offload, not per-call speed. At
   shallow depth, the enqueue cost makes rb slightly slower than
   global. At deep depth, the worker absorbs work that global runs
   inline, so rb's hot path stays roughly constant in depth while
   global's grows linearly.

--- Caveats ---

- The (64, 4) cell oversubscribes the 16 hardware threads on this
  machine 4x, so its baseline is dominated by scheduler behavior, not
  pthread fast path. Compare (N, 4) cells against each other within-
  condition; do not read absolute throughput across sweep rows as a
  parallel-scaling number.

- ns_per_pair[baseline] is *not* monotonic in either threads or depth.
  A single mutex word rewritten in a tight loop serializes the cmpxchg
  -> store -> load chain; spreading work across more cachelines lets
  the out-of-order window hide that latency. Treat overhead *ratios*
  as qualitative; treat marginal ns/pair as the trustworthy number.
