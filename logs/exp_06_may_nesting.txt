--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. The deep variant is the scenario lockdep is built for: 40 nested
   acquires per iteration, 40 threads, no actual deadlock. Each
   nested acquire under global mode walks the held-stack to add up to
   N edges, then runs DFS for cycle detection — work that is O(N) per
   acquire and O(N^2) per iteration. global pays 43 ns per pair on top
   of the 23 ns/pair baseline; that's 1.85x marginal, 2.85x total.

2. rb pushes that O(depth) work onto a single worker thread. On the
   deep workload, rb finishes 18% faster than global in wall time
   (878 ms vs 1066 ms) — the worker absorbs the graph work while the
   40 application threads only pay the enqueue cost. Marginal cost
   drops from 43 ns/pair (global) to 31 ns/pair (rb).

3. On the shallow workload, rb is ~5% slower than global. With only
   3 held locks, the O(depth) work is tiny — the predecessor-summary
   short-circuit makes the global mode's DFS essentially free after
   the first few iterations. rb still has to pay the per-acquire
   enqueue cost. The crossover where rb starts winning is somewhere
   around held-stack depth 8-16 on this CPU, which matches what the
   pedges experiment shows directly.

4. The deep baseline (23 ns/pair) is faster than the shallow baseline
   (78 ns/pair) because the deep run does 13x more pthread_mutex_lock
   calls in roughly 4x the wall time — there is more parallelism
   available across 40 distinct mutexes than across 3 shared mutexes,
   so cache-line contention is lower per acquire. Compare overhead
   *ratios*, not absolute baselines, between shallow and deep.
