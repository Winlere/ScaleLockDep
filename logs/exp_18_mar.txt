Baseline scales near-linearly (locks are independent, parallelism is
effective). Lockdep throughput collapses with thread count despite zero
application-level contention. The global g_meta_lock fully serializes
all lockdep metadata updates, turning an embarrassingly parallel
workload into a sequential one.

--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

The single global spinlock (g_meta_lock in state.c) protecting the
dependency graph and slot registries is the root cause of the
scalability failure. Overhead is not driven by lock contention in the
target application — it is driven purely by thread count. This makes
the current implementation unsuitable for workloads with many threads
and fine-grained locking.

The overhead factor grows super-linearly:
  threads:  1    2    4    8    16   32    64
  overhead: 4×   5×   7×   17×  41×  346×  592×

Primary target for ScaleLockDep: replace g_meta_lock with finer-grained
synchronization (e.g., per-lock-slot locks, sharded dependency graph, or
read-write locks separating graph reads from writes).
