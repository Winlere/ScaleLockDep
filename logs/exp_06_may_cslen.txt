--- Crossover ---

The crossover point — where lockdep overhead disappears under
application work — is between 30 and 50 ns of critical-section hold
time. At cs_ns >= 50, baseline and lockdep track within ~10% of each
other across both modes; at cs_ns >= 200 the difference is
indistinguishable from measurement noise.

--- Comparison with exp_20_apr_cslen.txt ---

The shape of the curve is identical to the April run on commit f760bcd:
- 0-30 ns: ~1.5-2x overhead (lockdep dominates the cycle budget)
- 50-200 ns: visible but modest overhead
- >=500 ns: lost in the noise floor
The rb column did not exist in the April log; it tracks global within
~10% throughout.

--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. lockdep adds a fixed metadata cost per lock/unlock pair (held-stack
   push/pop, dependency-edge bookkeeping, TLS cache hit). When the
   critical section does no work, that cost is the entire wall time
   delta and shows up as a 1.8-1.9x overhead. As the application puts
   real work inside the critical section, the metadata cost is amortized
   to zero.

2. The crossover at ~30-50 ns matches the per-pair overhead measured
   directly in the latency experiment (lockdep adds ~20 ns/pair at
   1 thread). Once application CS time exceeds the lockdep tax, the
   tax is invisible.

3. global vs rb track within ~10% across the sweep. With only one
   shared lock and a held-stack depth of 1, there is no offload-able
   O(depth) work for rb to push to its worker; the enqueue cost is
   paid for nothing. The two backends would diverge in rb's favor on
   this benchmark only if nesting depth were varied — which is what
   experiments 4 and 5 do.

4. Practical consequence: any workload where the typical critical
   section is more than a few cache misses long pays effectively no
   lockdep overhead. The high-overhead regime in this experiment
   (cs_ns < 30) is a microbenchmark artifact; real applications rarely
   take a mutex to do nothing.
