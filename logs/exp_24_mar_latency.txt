--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. Uncontended lockdep cost (1 thread): lockdep adds +23 ns to lock and
   +22 ns to unlock. This is the pure instrumentation cost — slot lookup,
   TLS held-lock push/pop, and g_meta_lock acquire/release with zero
   contention. Total pair overhead: 45 ns (2.2×).

2. Lock is consistently more expensive than unlock under lockdep. At 8
   threads, lock adds 1460 ns vs unlock adds 356 ns (4:1 ratio). This is
   because lock triggers: (a) trylock fast path, (b) if contended,
   before-blocking wait-chain walk, (c) after acquire, dependency edge
   insertion + DFS cycle check. Unlock only does: ownership clear + TLS
   pop.

3. Added delay grows super-linearly with threads. At 1 thread the added
   pair cost is 45 ns; at 64 threads it is 46,202 ns — a 1000× increase.
   This is driven by g_meta_lock spinlock contention: with 64 threads,
   each lock/unlock must spin-wait for the global metadata lock while 63
   other threads compete for it.

4. Lock delay dominates at high thread counts. At 64 threads, lockdep
   lock latency (46.6 us) is 5× the unlock latency (9.2 us). The lock
   path does more work under g_meta_lock (edge insertion, DFS) than the
   unlock path (ownership clear).

5. The baseline itself shows increasing latency with threads (18 ns → 8 us
   for lock at 1 → 64 threads) due to mutex contention. Lockdep amplifies
   this contention because it holds g_meta_lock across the metadata update,
   extending the effective critical section of every lock/unlock.
