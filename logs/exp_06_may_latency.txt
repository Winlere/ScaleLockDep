The 1-thread tax (~20 ns) is the pure interception cost: dlsym
indirection + recursive guard + held-stack push/pop + TLS lookup. As
threads grow, the absolute tax grows roughly linearly because every
contended acquire serializes on g_meta_lock (global) or contends for
the per-thread ring-buffer write index (rb).

--- Lock vs unlock split ---

At 1 thread the lock and unlock paths cost the same in lockdep
(~26 ns lock, ~30 ns unlock); at 64 threads, lock dominates massively
(~10-11 us lock vs ~2 us unlock) because acquire is the path that
runs before-blocking wait-chain detection while unlock just pops the
held-stack and decrements counters.

--- Comparison with exp_20_apr_latency.txt ---

April run (f760bcd, global only): 1T pair = 51.7 ns, 64T pair =
~13.5 us in global. Today's global = 56.1 ns / 12.9 us — the small
1-thread bump (+4 ns) is within run-to-run noise on clock_gettime.
No regression on the multi-thread end. rb is new; it tracks global
within 5% throughout.

--- What the Experiment Reveals ---

1. The 1-thread floor is ~20 ns of pure metadata cost per lock/unlock
   pair. This is the irreducible price of having lockdep present at
   all on the hot path.

2. Per-pair tax grows super-linearly with thread count under high
   contention because both backends serialize *some* state: global
   serializes on g_meta_lock, rb serializes on the worker's drain
   bandwidth (and on the per-thread ring write index, though that is
   single-producer). At 64 threads on 16 hardware threads the chip is
   already 4x oversubscribed on baseline — adding lockdep simply
   stretches the cmpxchg chain.

3. global and rb are within ~5% of each other for every cell because
   nesting depth is 1 (single shared lock). rb has no work to offload;
   it pays the enqueue cost to gain nothing. This experiment is the
   pessimal case for rb. Compare against the deep-nesting experiments
   for the case where rb earns its keep.

4. The asymmetry between lock and unlock latency at high thread counts
   is intrinsic to mutex contention, not a lockdep artifact: the
   blocking-acquire path is where threads queue, while unlock is a
   short atomic store and a wakeup hint. lockdep's wait-chain check
   in lockdep_before_blocking_mutex_lock tracks this asymmetry — it
   only runs when the trylock fails, which is exactly when there is
   already a queue.
